# LibreHR Trademark Policy

Version 1.0 · 2026-11-10

"LibreHR", the LibreHR logo and the "L'Arc" mark (the **Marks**) identify the official LibreHR project and the services of its Project Steward (as defined in `CLA.md`). The Marks are used as trademarks (™). Applications for registration may be filed, and ® will be used only once a registration is granted.

The code licences (AGPL-3.0-or-later and the LibreHR Enterprise License) cover copyright only. They grant **no** rights to the Marks. The CC BY-SA 4.0 licence on the brand image files (`docs/brand.md`) also covers only the copyright in those files. It grants no right to use the Marks as a trademark.

## Allowed without asking

- Saying truthfully that your product or service is "based on LibreHR", "compatible with LibreHR" or "a fork of LibreHR". The Marks must not be the most prominent name on your product.
- Using the Marks to refer to the project in articles, talks, tutorials, reviews and academic work.
- Running official releases for your own organisation under the LibreHR name, including with your own configuration and with patches that only fix bugs or security issues.
- Distributing official releases, including in Linux distributions and container registries. Changes needed for packaging and security fixes are allowed. You must not suggest an endorsement or partnership that does not exist.

## Needs written permission

- Offering LibreHR, or a modified version, to third parties as a hosted or managed service under the Marks (for example "LibreHR hosting"). Hosting providers and gestorías can apply to the partner programme; see `docs/editions.md`.
- Using the Marks, or a confusingly similar name, in a company, product, domain, app store listing or social media name.
- Merchandise, event names, certifications and training courses presented as official.

## Not allowed

- Distributing a version with functional changes under the Marks. Forks must use a different name and logo.
- Using the Marks in a way that suggests the project endorses you when it does not.
- Using the Marks in a misleading or disparaging way, or in breach of the law.
- Registering the Marks, or confusingly similar signs, as trademarks or domain names.

## General

- Permission is personal, non-transferable and revocable unless a written agreement says otherwise.
- The Project Steward may update this policy. The version published in the repository applies.
- To ask for permission, or to report misuse, contact the maintainers through the channel listed in `SECURITY.md`.
