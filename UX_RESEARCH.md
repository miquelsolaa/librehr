## Findings and design implications

- Many Spanish businesses are micro-firms. Test whether owners, office managers and external gestorias are the primary administrators and decision makers; do not treat market-size or software-adoption estimates as validated purchasing evidence.
- Digital skills vary. Design employee clock, leave and document flows for plain language, large targets, visible actions, forgiving errors and mobile use. Explore a non-smartphone path through a non-biometric shared-device kiosk.
- Working-time records must be understandable and trustworthy to employees as well as useful to employers. Show a person's own records, make corrections visible and communicate corrections; never silently change recorded hours or introduce productivity scores.
- Forgotten clock-ins and clock-outs warrant easy correction flows, clear reasons and a way to explain system outages. The relative frequency and effectiveness of reminders remain hypotheses.
- Leave rules and working-day calculations depend on policy, calendars and applicable agreements. Make balances explainable and allow authorized corrections with a reason. Do not treat a fixed working-day conversion or a 60-day product rule as a verified legal default.
- Inspection readiness motivates reliable records and export. The details of any pending digital-registry regulation are time-sensitive; recheck official sources before release and do not claim compliance with an unpublished requirement.
- Gestoria exports and mapping may be important to adoption, but the report identifies this as a high-priority research gap, not a finding from gestors.
- The report recommends no biometric clocking and suggests PIN, QR or card alternatives. Any formal change to compliance policy or system requirements must be checked against primary sources and the repository's approval rules.

## Unvalidated hypotheses and primary research

The submitted report identifies H1–H12: customer-size fit, relative importance of leave and clocking, employee trust, correction reminders, gestoria exports, self-hosting demand, balance explainability, shared-device needs, Catalan-language differentiation, sector presets, inspection readiness and AI feature priority. Its proposed first round includes owners or office managers, gestors, employees including non-desk workers, team managers and self-hosters, with consent and pseudonymous notes. The persona of a floor worker is proposed for validation; it is not based on an interview.

The report proposes updating PRD priorities, the screen inventory, compliance documentation and an ADR. These are **proposals**, not approved legal defaults or completed requirements. The provided report contains no primary user data; representative-user testing remains necessary before treating the proposed UX as validated.
